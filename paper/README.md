# Paper

**Status:** not started. No manuscript exists yet. No confirmatory results exist yet.
**Target:** a decision-theory + causal-measurement paper, not a "we measured the curve" paper.

## Thesis

The paper's object is the decision around the retrieval depth: the energy per correct answer
`E(k) = E[J_k]/E[C_k]` under a pre-registered accuracy gate, the optimal-depth identity, the
price of static depth, and the cost of certifying `k*`.

## What the paper may claim

- The energy-depth tradeoff is steep and measurable under the stated conditions.
- At a 5 pp EM non-inferiority margin, an interior depth (`k = 4`) saves a large fraction of
  energy — **if** the powered rerun supports it.
- The identity is a useful local diagnostic; it flags over-retrieval.
- Query-conditional depth is a promising direction; the oracle gap is an upper bound.

## What the paper may not claim

- A universal `k*` or a "2 pp optimal" depth.
- A "5 pp certified" result before the rerun and the freeze.
- A learned-policy gain before the P3 held-out evaluation.
- Carbon optimality beyond constant-factor reporting.

## Structure

1. Introduction: the decision, not the curve.
2. Related work: Pan et al., RAGMark, adaptive-k, Token Arena, Lost in the Middle.
3. Theory: Theorem 1 (identity, unimodality, comparative statics), Theorem 2 (price of static
   depth), Theorem 3 (certification rate and `N*`), the distractor counterexample.
4. Measurement protocol and harness.
5. Results: primary 5 pp, descriptive curves, identity, certification, sensitivity.
6. Replication and distractor.
7. Policy and oracle.
8. Energy-tax manipulation.
9. Limitations: one GPU, one corpus, one retriever, strict EM, power.
10. Supplement: configs, checksums, full tables, amendment log.
